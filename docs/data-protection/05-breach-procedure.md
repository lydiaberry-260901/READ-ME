# Data breach procedure

**Draft for legal review.** For everyone at Moca, and in detail for the data protection lead and admins.

## What counts as a breach

Any time personal data is lost, destroyed, changed, shared or accessed when it should not have been, **including by accident**. Examples:

* an email with contact details sent to the wrong person
* a laptop or phone with CRM access lost or stolen
* someone's account used by another person, or a password shared
* a CSV export left somewhere others can see it
* data deleted by mistake with no backup
* a supplier telling us they have had a breach

## 1. Report it straight away (everyone)

Tell the data protection lead at once: [name], [phone], [privacy email address]. If you cannot reach them, tell an admin. Do not wait until you know everything, and do not try to investigate on your own. Reporting quickly is never a problem; delay can be.

## 2. Record it and start the clock (lead or admin)

In the CRM: Privacy centre, Breaches, Report a breach. Enter when we found out. **This starts the 72 hour clock**: if the breach is likely to be a risk to people, the ICO must be told within 72 hours of Moca becoming aware of it. The clock is shown live on the breach page and the Privacy centre, and reminders are emailed.

## 3. Contain it

Act quickly to limit the harm: ask the wrong recipient to delete the email and confirm; reset passwords; switch off a user's access (People and teams); disconnect a lost device from Google or Microsoft; revoke a call recording tool key (Call recording tools). Tick "Contain it" on the checklist.

## 4. Assess it

Work out what data was involved, roughly how many people, and what could happen to them. Record it on the breach page. Then decide the risk:

* **Unlikely to be a risk to people:** for example a short list of work emails sent to a colleague who deleted it at once. Do not report to the ICO, but record why.
* **A risk to people:** report to the ICO within 72 hours.
* **A high risk to people:** report to the ICO, and tell the people affected without delay, in plain language, with what they can do to protect themselves.

## 5. Tell the ICO (if needed)

Report online through the ICO's breach reporting form, or by phone on 0303 123 1113. If you do not yet have every detail, report within 72 hours anyway and follow up. Record the decision, the reason and the ICO's reference in the CRM.

## 6. Tell the people affected (if high risk)

Explain what happened, the likely effects, what we have done, what they can do, and who to contact. Tick "The people affected have been told".

## 7. Close and learn

Record what was done and what will stop it happening again, then close the breach in the CRM. The record is kept whether or not it was reported, as the law requires. Review lessons at [the next team meeting].

## Suppliers

Our contracts with suppliers require them to tell us about breaches without delay. Treat their notice as the start of our clock, and follow this procedure.
