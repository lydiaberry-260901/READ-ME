# Procedure for requests from people about their data

**Draft for legal review.** For everyone at Moca, and in detail for the data protection lead and admins, who handle requests in the CRM (Privacy centre, Requests).

## 1. Recognising a request

A request can arrive by any route (email, phone, letter, social media, in person, or through a colleague) and does not have to use legal words or mention the law. Examples: "What do you hold on me?", "Take me off your list", "Delete my details", "Your records say I work at X, that's wrong".

**Anyone at Moca who receives one must pass it to the data protection lead the same day,** at [privacy email address]. Do not reply with personal data yourself.

If the request is only to stop marketing ("unsubscribe", "don't contact me"), you can also act on it straight away yourself: on the contact's page, use Opt out. This blocks all contact for everyone at once.

## 2. Recording it

The lead (or an admin) records it in the CRM, in Privacy centre, Requests, Record a request, with:

* what they are asking for (see, correct, delete, limit, object, or a copy)
* the date it was received, which starts the clock
* their name and how to reply
* their contact record, if we have one (search by name or email)

The CRM sets the deadline: **one calendar month**. For example, a request received on 3 September is due on 3 October, and one received on 31 January is due on the last day of February. If that falls at a weekend, the deadline is the next Monday. Bank holidays are not counted automatically, so check them.

Reminders are emailed to the lead and admins every morning at 08:00 from 7 days before the deadline.

## 3. Checking who they are

Only ask for what is reasonable. A request from the same work email address we hold is usually enough. If in doubt (for example a request from a personal address asking for a copy of everything), ask for something that confirms it is them. Tick "We have checked they are who they say they are" in the CRM. The deadline keeps running while you wait for an answer; if they do not reply, the request can be closed saying so.

## 4. Dealing with each type

**See their data, or receive a copy.** On the request page, use Download their data. This produces one file (in JSON, a format other systems can read) with everything linked to them: their details and where they came from, marketing checks, deals, calls, emails, meetings, tasks, transcripts and drafts. The page also lists other places their name appears in free text (notes, tasks, transcripts, deal fields). Check each one; include what is about them.

Before sending: read the file, and remove information about other people (for example another person's comments in a transcript) unless it is reasonable to include it. Send it securely to the address confirmed in step 3, with a short covering note explaining what the data is, why we hold it, where it came from, who we share it with and how long we keep it (the privacy notice covers this).

**Correct their data.** Open their contact record, correct it, then close the request saying what changed.

**Delete their data.** Close the request as done. The CRM deletes their contact record, emails, call transcripts, drafts and tasks about them; calls and meetings stay in the totals without anything identifying them. Leave "keep a minimal entry on the do not contact list" ticked unless they object to that too: it holds only a scrambled copy of their email and phone, so we never contact them again. Check the "other places their name appears" list and remove or correct those by hand. Deleted data leaves the backups within 14 days.

**Limit the use of their data.** Close the request as done. The CRM marks their details as limited, which blocks all emails, calls and invitations until the limit is lifted.

**Object.** For marketing, always comply: closing the request opts them out for everyone. For other use, stop unless there is a compelling reason not to, and record it.

## 5. More time, or refusing

* **More time:** for complex or numerous requests, the deadline can be extended by up to two further months. Record the reason in the CRM, and tell the person within the first month.
* **Refusing:** only if a request is clearly unfounded or excessive, or an exemption applies. Take advice first. Close it in the CRM as refused, with the reason, and tell the person why and that they can complain to the ICO.
* **No data held:** close the request saying so, and tell them.

## 6. Closing and records

Record what was done and when the person was told. The CRM keeps the request, its history and every download of their data. Normally there is no fee.
