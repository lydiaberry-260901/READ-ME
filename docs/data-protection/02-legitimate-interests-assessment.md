## What the processing is

Moca holds the business details of people at organisations that may benefit from its energy software for commercial property (asset and ESG managers, property managers, and occupiers with several sites). It uses them to research, contact and follow up those people by email and phone, and to manage relationships and deals. The details are held in the Moca CRM.

## 1. The purpose test: is there a legitimate interest?

* Moca's interest is finding and winning business customers, which is a recognised legitimate interest (direct marketing is named as one in the UK GDPR recitals).
* The software addresses real obligations and costs these organisations face: EPC and MEES rules, CRREM pathways, ESOS and SECR reporting, net zero commitments, grid capacity limits and recharging tenants for energy. Contact is relevant to the people's jobs.
* The benefit also reaches the organisations contacted (lower energy costs and clearer reporting) and wider society (lower carbon emissions from buildings).
* If Moca could not do this, it could not reach most of its market, as buyers in these roles rarely come forward unprompted.

## 2. The necessity test: is the processing necessary?

* Contacting the right person needs their name, job title and work contact details. Nothing more is collected: the CRM has no fields for personal addresses, personal phones or sensitive information, and notes fields warn against recording personal opinions or sensitive details.
* Less intrusive options were considered. Advertising alone does not reach the specific roles involved. Contacting organisations only through general inboxes rarely reaches the decision maker.
* Company level information (the AI summary and score) is about organisations, not people.

## 3. The balancing test: do people's interests override Moca's?

**Reasonable expectations.** People in these roles commonly receive relevant business approaches about their area of work. Details come from business and public sources connected with their job.

**Likely impact.** Low: the details are business contact details, the contact is about the person's work, and they can stop it at any time with one click or a reply.

**Vulnerable people.** Not expected: contacts are professionals acting in a work capacity.

**Individuals treated as consumers.** Sole traders and some partnerships are treated as individuals under PECR. The CRM records each contact's organisation type and blocks marketing emails to these contacts unless consent is recorded.

## Safeguards

* Business details only, with the source and date recorded for every contact.
* Who Moca is, why it holds the details, where they came from and how to object are included in the first email, and within one month at the latest (the CRM tracks this).
* A working unsubscribe link in every marketing email. Opt outs apply straight away for every user, and are kept on a minimal do not contact list (hashed) that survives deletion.
* Phone numbers checked against the TPS and CTPS before cold calls, with the check date recorded and a fresh check required after 28 days.
* Contacts with no activity for 24 months are listed for deletion each month, and deleted once an admin approves.
* Access by role: reps see their own and shared records, managers their team's, admins everything. Views and downloads of personal data are logged.
* AI is used for drafts and summaries only. Email addresses and phone numbers are removed first, a person reviews everything, and nothing is sent automatically.
* Requests from people about their data are tracked against a one month deadline.
